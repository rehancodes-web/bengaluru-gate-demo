Operate the model



One step = 1 simulated second. Run advances 10 simulated seconds per real second. Changing the case resets the reservoir levels and alarm counters.

How the controller chooses the opening

Both stage transmitters measure against one surveyed datum. The simulated controller enters flood mode at 1.60 m upstream and exits at 1.55 m. While active it requests the estimated lake inflow plus a correction to return toward 1.55 m over 300 s. It limits that release using downstream conveyance and available downstream storage.

Demand = max(0, lake inflow + 500 × (upstream stage − 1.55) / 300). Receiving allowance = max(0, downstream outflow − local downstream inflow + 400 × (1.80 − downstream stage) / 120). All flows are in m³/s.

Target opening = 100 × min(demand, allowance) / full-opening flow, limited to 0–100%. For forward submerged discharge, full-opening flow = 0.62 × 2.0 × 0.50 × √(2 × 9.81 × stage difference). The gate travels at up to 2 percentage points per second. At downstream stage ≥1.80 m or forward head ≤0.05 m, the model requests closure. Travel takes time, so actual discharge can persist or reverse.

Worked example. Select “Lake rising” and press Reset. Upstream 1.85 m, downstream 1.10 m and lake inflow 0.50 m³/s give 1.00 m³/s demand. Full-opening flow is 2.38 m³/s. The initial target is 42% (0.21 m lift). Click Advance to see actual travel start at 2%.

Assumed tank areas are 500 and 400 m². Downstream outflow is 1.2 × max(stage − 0.60, 0) m³/s, reduced to 15% in the blocked-outlet case. Both simulated overflow crests are at 2.20 m. Excess volume is counted as spill. Inflows are scenario inputs. A real controller would need validated inflow estimates. Below the submerged model range (either stage <0.50 m), automatic regulation is suspended and gate discharge is unmodelled.

Which flood mechanisms can the outlet influence?

MechanismGate response and complementary works
Lake filling with a clear receiving channelMeter release within receiving capacity. Verify downstream peak, timing and duration.
Receiving river or drain already highLimit release and detect reversal. Continued local runoff requires detention, a verified discharge route or engineered pumping.
Blocked culvert / upstream street pondingInvestigate differential levels and inspect the reach. Clearing a restriction or improving inlets can restore capacity independently of gate control.
Rainfall exceeds combined lake and channel capacityTrack spill and warn operators. Assess additional detention, overflow routing and downstream warning.
Disconnected street basin, sewer surcharge or groundwater floodingA lake outlet has no direct control without a hydraulic connection. Investigate surface drainage, sewer capacity or groundwater pathways separately.

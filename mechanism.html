Build envelope and mechanical interfaces

Build a gravity-fed, clean-water bench rig with a 100 × 50 mm port at the bottom of a divider. The 50 mm actuator stroke matches the port height. Use two hand-filled chambers and a catch tray for the first tests. This lets you measure head and leakage without introducing a recirculation pump into the experiment.

Download the parametric cartridge model (.scad). Open in OpenSCAD to adjust lift, isolate the leaf, mirrored guides or sill, and export the divider drilling template. Hole coordinates use the sill as the vertical datum. Dry-fit and transfer-drill the purchased actuator mounts.

Commission the bench rig

Download the UNO bench checkout sketch (.ino). Open in Arduino IDE in a folder named GateCommissioning, select UNO R4 WiFi and install the Arduino Servo library. With the linkage unpinned, use Serial Monitor at 115200 baud with Newline. Each data row gives elapsed milliseconds, upstream ADC and downstream ADC. Send PULSE 1500 for mid-stroke or OFF to detach the signal. A 60 s interval separates moves. Use the external disconnect to isolate power. This sketch supports attended calibration; automatic release decisions remain in the simulator.

  1. Align and pull-test. Unpin the actuator. Measure the peak force needed to start and move the leaf in both directions with a spring scale, dry and at the intended head. Record seal preload and repeat after wetting. The selected actuator lists 17 N at peak efficiency and 42 N maximum load. Adopt 8 N peak measured pull as a conservative prototype acceptance target. Investigate binding before increasing drive force.
  2. Check travel with the linkage free. Use the manufacturer's RC command range of 1–2 ms. Retraction raises this gate. Start at mid-stroke, approach limits incrementally, then pin the linkage and calibrate the usable limits so the servo never pushes against a hard stop. Observe the manufacturer's duty-cycle limit. L12 data sheet
  3. Calibrate the sensors. Fill each chamber separately and log ADC readings beside a ruler from the strip's active threshold to the maximum test depth. Check at five increasing levels, then repeat while draining. Store each sensor's calibration and valid interval. A reading below its active range cannot distinguish shallow levels.
  4. Verify commanded opening. Drive 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100% commands and record measured lift in mm on opening and closing. Ideal values are 0, 12.5, 25, 37.5 and 50 mm. Initial project target ±2 mm on three cycles, without binding. Record supply current and movement time.
  5. Measure hydraulic response. Keep downstream water below upstream water. Record both stages, lift and collected volume over a timed window. Divide litres by seconds for L/s. Repeat at multiple heads and lifts. Measure zero-command leakage separately and record its allowable value before testing.
  6. Validate automatic operation. First issue opening targets in a dry run with the motor disconnected. Use calibrated bench heads and a measured flow-versus-lift table; the simulator's metre-scale thresholds belong to its virtual reservoirs. Then test rising upstream stage, high downstream stage, unplugged sensor, and power interruption. Verify controlled restart and record actual lift against the pointer. Establish an independent position transducer before unattended tests.

Field selection needs a site load case. Survey sill and design flood levels on both sides, port width and height, debris and grit, required travel time, leakage class, power availability and duty cycle. Specify stem thrust, buckling margin, guides, anchorage, enclosure, independent limits and manual operation against those inputs with the responsible engineers.
